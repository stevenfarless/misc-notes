I lean toward classical cessationism. By that, I mean I believe certain miraculous sign gifts, particularly prophecy, tongues, and gifts of healing and miracles, served a foundational purpose in authenticating God's revelation and establishing the early church. I do not believe God has stopped performing miracles, answering prayers, or working supernaturally through His Spirit.

Consider the broader biblical context. Hebrews 1:1–2 describes how God spoke formerly through the prophets but has now spoken to us through His Son. Christ is the culmination and supreme revelation of God. Hebrews 2:3–4 then describes how the gospel, first announced by Christ and attested by those who heard Him, was confirmed through signs, wonders, miracles, and gifts of the Holy Spirit. Paul similarly identifies signs and wonders as credentials of his apostleship (2 Corinthians 12:12).

Ephesians 2:20 describes the apostles and prophets as the church's foundation, with Christ as its cornerstone. The church continues to build upon that established foundation. Miracles performed by people other than apostles, such as Stephen and Philip, do not invalidate this connection. They demonstrate that the signs accompanied the spread of the apostolic gospel beyond the apostles themselves.

I recognize that Scripture never explicitly gives an expiration date for these gifts. However, their connection to the confirmation of revelation and the foundational apostolic ministry provides a reason to understand them as having a particular historical purpose. I believe that is the most coherent explanation of their role in the New Testament.

I also recognize the major continuationist passages. Acts 2:17–39 describes the outpouring of the Spirit in the last days, extending the promise to future generations and everyone the Lord calls. I affirm that promise. The question is whether the Spirit's continuing presence requires every manifestation associated with the inauguration of the new covenant to continue throughout its entire duration. The promise of salvation and the distribution of particular spiritual gifts are related, but they are not identical claims.

First Corinthians 12–14 presents another serious argument. Paul commands believers to pursue spiritual gifts, especially prophecy, and explains that they are given for the common good. I accept those instructions as authoritative. However, instructions governing the church's use of gifts establish how they were to function when present; they do not, by themselves, establish how long every gift was intended to remain.

First Corinthians 13:8–12 is also frequently cited. The references to seeing face to face and knowing fully give continuationists a substantial argument for associating the passage's ultimate fulfillment with Christ's return. I don't believe cessationism needs to depend on claiming that "the perfect" means the completed Bible. Even if the passage describes Christ's return, it does not independently settle whether every gift must continue until then. Their purpose and relationship to the apostolic foundation must also be considered.

An important distinction is that God performing a miracle and an individual possessing a miraculous spiritual gift are two different questions. I believe God still heals, answers prayer, and intervenes supernaturally. I simply don't believe that every extraordinary event establishes the presence of a particular New Testament gift.

Modern claims of prophecy and tongues should also be examined against their biblical descriptions. Some continuationists describe prophecy as a fallible impression that must be weighed by the congregation. I question whether Scripture supports treating a potentially mistaken impression as a genuine message from God. Sincerity and personal experience alone cannot settle that question.

Ultimately, I believe the biblical evidence points to miraculous sign gifts serving a particular foundational role in the establishment of the church. The Holy Spirit remains active, the church continues to benefit from spiritual gifts, and God remains fully capable of performing miracles. I believe the apostolic foundation has been laid and those particular sign gifts have fulfilled their purpose.
