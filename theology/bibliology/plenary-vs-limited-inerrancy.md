## Domain & Evidence

Both positions affirm that Scripture is the word of God, that it is divinely inspired in a sense that distinguishes it from all other literature, that it carries unique authority over Christian faith and practice, and that Jesus Christ treated the Old Testament Scriptures as authoritative and trustworthy. Both reject the liberal position that Scripture is merely a human religious document with no special divine origin.

The dispute is internal to evangelical and conservative Protestant scholarship. It concerns the scope and nature of Scripture's inerrancy: whether the absence of error extends to everything Scripture affirms (including incidental historical, geographical, and scientific details) or whether it extends only to what Scripture intends to teach regarding salvation, theology, and ethics.

This document is the methodological foundation for every other debate in this repo. Every argument in the Soteriology, Eschatology, Anthropology, Theology Proper, and Ecclesiology documents bottoms out in a claim about what a text says, what it intends to affirm, and how confidently the interpreter can trust its details. 

The live disagreements are specific: what *theopneustos* (God-breathed, 2 Timothy 3:16) entails about the product of inspiration, how to interpret the phenomena of Scripture (numerical discrepancies, divergent parallel accounts, apparent historical errors), whether applying post-Enlightenment precision standards to ancient genres is an anachronistic category error, and how to handle the distinction between what Scripture asserts via divine accommodation versus what it affirms as binding truth.

## Key Definitions

### Plenary Inerrancy

**Position A, plenary inerrancy:** Scripture, in its original autographs, is without error in everything it affirms, because it is the product of divine inspiration extending to the words themselves (verbal plenary inspiration). Inerrancy is not limited to matters of faith and practice; it extends to historical, geographical, and scientific assertions where Scripture makes them. The Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978) is the definitive modern formulation. 

**Position B, limited inerrancy:** plenary inerrancy imposes on Scripture a standard of post-Enlightenment precision that the biblical authors did not intend and that the phenomena of Scripture do not support; it forces harmonistic interpretations that are implausible and creates an apologetic liability when the harmonizations fail.

### Limited Inerrancy / Infallibility

**Position B, limited inerrancy:** Scripture is fully authoritative and without error in everything it intends to teach regarding salvation, theology, and ethics. Its truthfulness is functional and purposive: the Bible accomplishes without failure the purpose for which God inspired it. Incidental details of history, geography, numbers, and cosmology reflect the knowledge conventions of the human authors' time (Divine Accommodation) without undermining the Bible's theological authority. 

**Position A, plenary inerrancy:** the limited inerrancy position provides no principled boundary between incidental errors and theological errors; the distinction between what Scripture intends to teach and what it merely accommodates is determined by the interpreter, not by the text, which gives the interpreter authority to exempt any inconvenient assertion from the scope of Scripture's truthfulness.

### Divine Accommodation and Speech Act Theory

**Position B:** Speech Act Theory distinguishes between the locution (the words used) and the illocutionary act (the intended effect or teaching). God accommodated His revelation to the ancient Near Eastern cosmological and historiographical frameworks of the human authors because His illocutionary act was theological formation, not scientific or mathematical instruction.

**Position A:** While God certainly accommodated His revelation to human language, He did not accommodate it to human *error*. God can speak in simple terms without speaking in false terms. Asserting that God utilized flawed historical or scientific frameworks as a vehicle for truth violates the deductive entailment of His perfect truthfulness.

### The Autographs Limitation

**Position A:** inerrancy applies to the original autographs, the manuscripts produced by the biblical authors, not to any subsequent copy or translation. This reflects the straightforward fact that inspiration attaches to the act of divinely superintended authorship, not to the subsequent process of transmission.

**Position B:** the autographs limitation renders inerrancy practically unfalsifiable and therefore theologically vacuous. Any apparent error in any extant manuscript can always be attributed to copyist error rather than to the original text, stripping the doctrine of any testable epistemic value.

## Foundational Assumptions

### Position A assumes

Plenary inerrantists begin with **the theological entailment from inspiration to inerrancy**. If God is the ultimate author of Scripture and God cannot lie or err (Numbers 23:19; Titus 1:2; Hebrews 6:18), then whatever God produces as his word cannot contain error. The argument is deductive: the character of the author determines the character of the product. 

A further assumption is that **the distinction between incidental and intentional assertions is not available to the interpreter without a principle external to the text**. If the interpreter decides which assertions Scripture intends to make and which are merely incidental, the interpreter rather than the text becomes the authority.

### Position B assumes

Limited inerrantists begin with **the phenomena of Scripture as a data set that any adequate doctrine of inspiration must account for**. The biblical texts contain numerical discrepancies between parallel accounts, differences in the wording of the same speech across the Gospels, and geocentric cosmological assumptions. 

A further assumption is that **the purpose of inspiration determines its scope**. God inspired Scripture to accomplish a specific purpose: to make human beings wise for salvation through faith in Christ (2 Timothy 3:15). Inerrancy in matters of ancient Near Eastern chronology is not necessary for that purpose, and claiming that inspiration guarantees it extends inspiration beyond its stated, functional aim.

# Position A: Plenary Inerrancy: Strongest Case

---

## Argument 1: The Self-Witness of Scripture to Its Own Truthfulness

Scripture does not merely claim authority; it claims truth. Psalm 119:160 states: "The sum of your word is truth, and every one of your righteous rules endures forever." Proverbs 30:5 states: "Every word of God proves true." John 17:17 has Jesus praying: "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth." 

These are not claims that Scripture is true in a limited or functional sense; they are unqualified claims about Scripture's truth. The Psalmist does not say "the salvific portions of your word are truth"; he says the sum is truth and every word proves true. The self-witness of Scripture to its own truthfulness does not allow the interpreter to introduce a category of incidental error.

> **P1:** Psalm 119:160 and Proverbs 30:5 make unqualified claims about the truthfulness of God's word, without distinguishing salvific from incidental assertions.
>
> **P2:** John 17:17 identifies God's word with truth as such, not with a subset of truth limited to theological subject matter.
>
> **P3:** Introducing a category of incidental errors that fall outside the scope of Scripture's truthfulness requires overriding the unqualified scope of Scripture's own self-witness.
>
> **C:** Scripture's self-witness to its own truthfulness is unqualified; limiting inerrancy to intended theological assertions imports a distinction that the texts do not support.

## Argument 2: Jesus' Use of Scripture Treats Its Details as Authoritative

Jesus treats Old Testament texts with a precision that extends to individual words and grammatical details. In Matthew 22:31-32, his argument about the resurrection turns on the present tense of "I am the God of Abraham" rather than "I was." In John 10:35, he argues from the fact that the Old Testament called certain human judges "gods," and adds: "Scripture cannot be broken." 

These examples show that Jesus' confidence in Scripture extends to individual words and grammatical forms, not merely to the overall theological message. If Jesus treats individual grammatical details as carrying authoritative weight, a doctrine of inerrancy limited only to macro-theological assertions does not match his practice.

> **P1:** In Matthew 22:31-32, Jesus builds a theological argument on the present tense of a single verb in Exodus 3:6.
>
> **P2:** In John 10:35, Jesus argues from a single word in Psalm 82:6 and adds the categorical statement "Scripture cannot be broken."
>
> **P3:** In Matthew 5:18, Jesus ties the permanence of Scripture to its individual letters and punctuation marks.
>
> **C:** Jesus' argumentative practice treats the details of Scripture as carrying authoritative weight, which is consistent with plenary verbal inerrancy and inconsistent with limited inerrancy.

## Argument 3: The Entailment from Divine Authorship

The argument runs from the character of God to the character of his word. God cannot lie (Titus 1:2; Numbers 23:19; Hebrews 6:18). Scripture is the product of divine inspiration, meaning God is its ultimate author (2 Timothy 3:16; 2 Peter 1:20-21). If God is the author and God cannot lie, Scripture cannot contain falsehood. 

This argument does not require that inspiration was dictation; it requires only that God's superintendence of the process was effective enough that the product reflects his character. A God who inspired a text that contains errors, even incidental ones, either did not effectively superintend the process or does not hold the truthfulness of incidental historical details to be part of his own character. 

> **P1:** Titus 1:2 and Numbers 23:19 state that God cannot lie; his character is constitutively truthful in all assertions.
>
> **P2:** 2 Timothy 3:16 attributes Scripture's origin to God's breath, making God the ultimate author.
>
> **P3:** The character of the author determines the character of the product.
>
> **C:** The deductive entailment from God's truthfulness requires no additional premise beyond the inspiration claim itself; limited inerrancy breaks the entailment by allowing that God effectively produced a text containing errors.

---

## Position A -- Critique of Position B

### Critique 1: The Affirmation-Assumption Distinction Transfers Authority to the Interpreter

Limited inerrancy depends on distinguishing what Scripture intends to affirm from what it merely assumes or accommodates. The Bible intends to teach salvation; it does not intend to teach ancient Near Eastern chronology, so errors in those domains do not impugn its truthfulness. 

The problem is that this distinction is made by the interpreter, not by the text. The text does not signal which of its assertions are intentional affirmations and which are incidental assumptions. The interpreter applies external criteria to sort the assertions, which means the interpreter's judgment rather than the text's authority determines what Scripture teaches.

> **P1:** The distinction between intended affirmations and incidental assumptions is not marked in the text; it is applied by the interpreter using criteria external to the text.
>
> **P2:** If the interpreter determines which assertions fall within the scope of inerrancy and which do not, the interpreter rather than the text becomes the effective authority.
>
> **P3:** A doctrine of scriptural authority that subordinates the text's claims to the interpreter's categorization has undermined the authority it was meant to establish.
>
> **C:** Limited inerrancy's affirmation-assumption distinction transfers epistemic authority from Scripture to the interpreter.

### Critique 2: The Slope from Limited Inerrancy to Broader Erosion Is Historically Documented

Harold Lindsell documented in *The Battle for the Bible* (1976) that institutions and scholars who abandoned plenary inerrancy consistently moved toward positions that were more theologically liberal over the following decades. 

The mechanism is straightforward: once the interpreter is empowered to identify incidental errors in Scripture, the category of the incidental expands. What begins as accommodation to ancient cosmology extends to accommodation to ancient moral assumptions, which extends to accommodation to ancient views on gender, sexuality, and atonement. The limiting principle has no stopping point because the affirmation-assumption distinction applies wherever the interpreter judges the text to be out of step with contemporary knowledge.

> **P1:** Identifying the category of incidental error grants the interpreter a tool with no principled limiting boundary.
>
> **P2:** Historical documentation shows that institutions departing from plenary inerrancy moved toward broader theological liberalism over subsequent decades.
>
> **P3:** The category of the incidental expands with changing cultural and intellectual pressures.
>
> **C:** Limited inerrancy has no principled stopping point; the historical record shows the category of incidental error inevitably expanding into theological content.

---

## Position A -- Rebuttals to Position B

### Rebuttal 1: The Phenomena Objection Revisited

Limited inerrantists argue that the phenomena of Scripture (discrepancies, divergent parallels) force a limited inerrancy position. Plenary inerrantists respond that none of the standard phenomena objections are decisive. 

Numerical discrepancies between parallel accounts are typically textual transmission issues, rounding conventions, or different counting methods rather than authorial errors. Divergent wording of the same speech in the Synoptics reflects the ancient historiographical convention of reproducing the *substance* of a speech rather than a verbatim transcript. None of these phenomena require positing authorial error; they require attending to the literary conventions of the genres and cultures involved, which plenary inerrancy explicitly accommodates.

> **P1:** Numerical discrepancies are typically attributable to textual transmission, rounding, or different counting methods.
>
> **P2:** Divergent wording of speeches reflects ancient biographical conventions of reproducing the substance of a speech, not a verbatim transcript.
>
> **P3:** Plenary inerrancy acknowledges and incorporates these ancient literary conventions (as seen in the Chicago Statement).
>
> **C:** The phenomena of Scripture are explicable within a plenary inerrancy framework by attending to genre without positing authorial error.

# Position B: Limited Inerrancy / Infallibility: Strongest Case

---

## Argument 1: Divine Accommodation and Speech Act Theory

The strongest academic case for limited inerrancy relies on Divine Accommodation and Speech Act Theory. Speech Act Theory demonstrates that the illocutionary force (the intended meaning) of a communication is distinct from its locutionary framework (the words and cultural assumptions used). 

God accommodated His revelation to the ancient Near Eastern cosmological and historiographical frameworks of the human authors because His illocutionary act was theological formation, not scientific instruction. God used the concept of a solid heavenly firmament (the *raqia* in Genesis 1) because that was the shared cosmological understanding of the ancient Near East. God accommodated the message to their framework to teach them that He is the sole Creator, not to teach them modern astrophysics. 

> **P1:** Speech Act Theory distinguishes between the intended teaching of a text and the cultural/linguistic framework used to deliver it.
>
> **P2:** God accommodated His revelation to ancient cosmological and historiographical frameworks to effectively communicate theological truth to ancient audiences.
>
> **P3:** The presence of an ancient, scientifically outdated framework is a feature of divine accommodation, not an "error" in the theological intent.
>
> **C:** Scripture is entirely truthful in its illocutionary act (what it intends to teach) without requiring its accommodated locutionary framework to match modern scientific or historical precision.

## Argument 2: The Anachronism of Post-Enlightenment Precision Standards

Holding ancient texts to post-Enlightenment standards of journalistic, mathematical, or scientific precision is an anachronistic category error. Therefore, labeling an ancient cosmological framework or an approximated census number a "factual error" imposes a modern rubric on an ancient genre.

When a biblical author rounds an army's size from 18,342 to 20,000, or when parallel accounts synthesize timelines differently, these are not "errors" waiting to be harmonized; they are the correct, expected functioning of ancient historiography. Plenary inerrancy creates a false crisis by demanding that ancient texts behave like modern, post-Enlightenment encyclopedias, and then forcing unnatural harmonizations when they inevitably don't.

> **P1:** Ancient historiography and literature operated with very different conventions regarding precision, chronology, and quotation than modern post-Enlightenment literature.
>
> **P2:** Judging ancient texts by modern standards of exactitude is a categorical and historical anachronism.
>
> **P3:** Labeling these ancient conventions as "errors" (or forcing complex harmonizations to avoid calling them errors) misunderstands how truth was communicated in the ancient world.
>
> **C:** Limited inerrancy respects the ancient genres of Scripture by refusing to impose anachronistic standards of modern precision onto them.

## Argument 3: The Phenomena of Scripture Require a Limited Account

The biblical texts present phenomena that resist harmonization without implausible strain. 2 Samuel 24:1 states that God incited David to number Israel; 1 Chronicles 21:1 attributes the same incitement to Satan. 2 Kings 8:26 gives Ahaziah's age as 22 when he became king; 2 Chronicles 22:2 gives it as 42. Matthew 27:9-10 attributes a quotation to Jeremiah that is actually from Zechariah. 

The harmonizations proposed for these passages require positing undocumented copyist errors, alternative counting methods, and interpretive maneuvers that are cumulatively implausible. A doctrine of inspiration that generates a permanent apologetic project of unnatural harmonization is driven by a prior philosophical commitment, not by reading the texts as they actually present themselves.

> **P1:** Specific textual phenomena present apparent factual, numerical, and attributional discrepancies.
>
> **P2:** The harmonizations proposed for these texts require cumulative implausible maneuvers.
>
> **P3:** A doctrine that requires permanent harmonistic apologetics for phenomena that read naturally as minor errors is under perpetual evidential pressure.
>
> **C:** The phenomena of Scripture fit more naturally with a limited inerrancy that treats incidental discrepancies as genuine minor errors caused by human authors.

## Position B -- Critique of Position A

### Critique 1: Plenary Inerrancy Conflates Infallibility and Inerrancy

The category of infallibility, that Scripture unfailingly accomplishes its purpose, is distinct from inerrancy, that Scripture contains no false assertion of any kind. Infallibility is a claim about Scripture's efficacy; inerrancy is a claim about its propositional accuracy across all disciplines. 

The pre-modern tradition affirmed infallibility and used language about Scripture's truthfulness that was oriented toward its saving function. Warfield's innovation was to define inerrancy in terms of mathematical and historical propositional accuracy in all subject matters, which is a more precise and demanding claim than the historic tradition required. 

> **P1:** Infallibility (Scripture achieves its saving purpose) and inerrancy (Scripture contains no false proposition in any field) are distinct claims.
>
> **P2:** The pre-modern tradition's language about Scripture's truthfulness was primarily oriented toward its saving efficacy, not post-Enlightenment propositional accuracy.
>
> **P3:** Warfield's formulation of inerrancy is a more demanding claim than the tradition required.
>
> **C:** Plenary inerrancy as the Chicago Statement defines it is a stronger claim than the historic tradition requires; limited inerrancy is the more historically grounded position.

### Critique 2: The Deductive Argument from Divine Authorship Is Too Strong

Plenary inerrantists argue: God cannot lie; God authored Scripture; therefore Scripture cannot contain error. Limited inerrantists respond that the argument proves too much. 

If the logic holds, it should apply to every word spoken by God's servants under his inspiration, including the speeches of Job's friends (which God explicitly says are not right, Job 42:7) and the reported speech of Satan (Genesis 3:4-5). Scripture accurately reports false assertions. The deductive argument must be refined to apply to the author's own *intended* assertions, not to everything contained in the text, which is precisely the distinction limited inerrancy uses.

> **P1:** Scripture contains reported speech that is explicitly false (Satan's lie, the wrong counsel of Job's friends).
>
> **P2:** The deductive argument from divine authorship cannot apply to these contents without making God the author of false assertions.
>
> **P3:** The deductive argument must therefore be restricted to the author's intended affirmations, not to everything contained in the text.
>
> **C:** Plenary inerrancy already implicitly uses the intended-assertion distinction to handle reported speech; limiting inerrancy is simply a consistent application of the same principle.

## Position B -- Rebuttals to Position A

### Rebuttal 1: The Slope Argument Is a Sociological Observation, Not a Logical Entailment

The plenary inerrantist claim that limited inerrancy leads to broader liberalism is a sociological observation, not a logical argument. The slope from limited inerrancy to theological liberalism is contingently correlated in certain institutional histories, but it is not logically required. 

Many limited inerrantists including I. Howard Marshall, F.F. Bruce, and R.P. Martin maintained orthodox Christology, the bodily resurrection, and substitutionary atonement alongside their position on Scripture. The limiting principle expands when interpreters who have *already* departed from orthodox theology use it as a tool. 

> **P1:** The logical entailment from limited inerrancy to theological liberalism does not hold; many limited inerrantists maintain full orthodox theology.
>
> **P2:** The institutional slide from limited inerrancy to liberalism occurs when interpreters who have already departed from orthodoxy use the framework as a tool.
>
> **P3:** The prior theological departure is the cause, not the limited inerrancy framework itself.
>
> **C:** The slippery slope argument is a sociological generalization; limited inerrancy does not logically require theological liberalism.

### Rebuttal 2: Jesus' Use of Scripture Demonstrates Theological Reliability, Not Plenary Precision

Plenary inerrantists argue that Jesus' precise use of Old Testament details (present tense verbs, individual words) implies verbal plenary inerrancy. Limited inerrantists respond that Jesus' use of Scripture demonstrates its authority and reliability for the theological purposes he employed it; it does not imply that every incidental historical detail elsewhere in the text is errorless. 

Jesus' argument in Matthew 22 turns on what Exodus teaches about the nature of God's relationship to the patriarchs. Using a text's grammatical detail to establish a theological point demonstrates that Scripture's theological assertions are reliable enough to bear argumentative weight; it does not require that every numerical figure in every historical narrative is errorless.

> **P1:** Jesus' precise use of Old Testament details demonstrates the reliability of Scripture's theological assertions for his arguments.
>
> **P2:** The scope of his confidence is the scope of his argument: theological truth regarding resurrection, not incidental historical trivia.
>
> **P3:** Using a grammatical detail to establish a theological point does not imply that every incidental historical detail in the entire canon is errorless.
>
> **C:** Jesus' use of Scripture demonstrates its reliability for theological argumentation; it does not mandate plenary inerrancy across all scientific and historical domains.

---

## The Three Actual Hinges

The first hinge is **the affirmation-assumption distinction and who controls it**. Plenary inerrancy argues that no principled line exists between intended affirmations and incidental assumptions without giving the interpreter total control over the scope of Scripture's authority. Limited inerrancy argues that Speech Act Theory and Divine Accommodation provide principled criteria for drawing the line between the intended teaching and the accommodated framework used to deliver it. 

The second hinge is **whether judging ancient texts by modern standards of precision is a category error**. Plenary inerrantists maintain confidence that harmonizations exist and that God's truthfulness extends to all domains. Limited inerrantists hold that applying post-Enlightenment precision standards to ancient historiography is deeply anachronistic, and that forcing harmonizations creates an unnecessary apologetic crisis. 

The third hinge is **the autographs limitation and whether it is theologically meaningful or apologetically vacuous**. Plenary inerrantists argue it is a precise claim about the scope of divine authorship. Limited inerrantists argue that a doctrine of Scripture's truthfulness that can never be empirically tested against any accessible document is performing no functional epistemic role.
