## Domain & Evidence

Both positions affirm the full inspiration and authority of Scripture, the universal reality of human sinfulness, the necessity of divine grace for salvation, the atoning work of Christ, and the final judgment of all persons. The dispute is not about whether grace is needed or whether Christ's death accomplishes salvation. It concerns the mechanism, scope, and priority of grace in the order of salvation.

Both employ literal-grammatical-historical hermeneutics in principle. Calvinists read election, predestination, and divine sovereignty texts as controlling, treating passages about God's sovereign choice as the interpretive anchor for texts about human response. Arminians treat passages about God's universal salvific will and the genuine conditionality of human response as controlling, reading election texts through the lens of foreknowledge rather than unconditional individual selection.

The relevant evidence is the text of Scripture itself: the Pauline treatments of election and predestination in Romans 8–9 and Ephesians 1, the universal-will passages in 1 Timothy 2:4 and 2 Peter 3:9, John's Gospel texts on drawing and coming, the perseverance and warning texts of Hebrews, and the narrative patterns of Acts and the Synoptics. Historical theology matters secondarily, since both traditions can trace their lineages to Augustine and to patristic sources.

The live disputes are specific: whether election in Romans 8–9 is individual and unconditional or corporate and conditional on foreseen faith, what the extent of the atonement was, whether total depravity removes the capacity for libertarian response or only a meritorious one, and whether the Spirit's regenerating work is resistible or irresistible.

## Key Definitions

### Total Depravity

**Position A, Calvinism:** sin has extended to every faculty of the human person — intellect, will, and affections — such that the unregenerate person cannot and will not choose God without the prior, effectual work of regenerating grace. Depravity does not mean every person is as sinful as possible, but that no faculty is untouched and none is capable of contributing to salvation.

**Position B, Arminianism:** sin has corrupted human nature thoroughly and genuinely, but prevenient grace — universally given through Christ — restores sufficient ability to respond to the gospel. The corruption is real; the resulting incapacity is addressed by grace prior to and apart from election.

### Unconditional Election

**Position A:** God chose specific individuals for salvation before the foundation of the world, on the basis of his sovereign will alone, not on the basis of any foreseen faith or merit in those individuals.

**Position B:** God's election is conditional on foreseen faith; he chooses those whom he foreknows will respond to the offer of the gospel. The initiative remains with God, but the condition is the person's free response.

### Definite (Limited) Atonement

**Position A:** Christ's atoning work was designed and effectually accomplished for the elect alone; it is sufficient for all but applied to the elect. This is the most disputed point among Calvinists; many four-point Calvinists affirm universal sufficiency without denying particular design.

**Position B:** Christ died for all persons without exception; the atonement provides a genuine basis for the salvation of every person who hears the gospel. Its effectiveness is conditioned on faith.

### Irresistible Grace

**Position A:** the Spirit's regenerating call, given to the elect, effectually produces faith; it cannot be ultimately resisted. The Spirit overcomes the resistance of the will and transforms it so that the elect genuinely and freely choose what the Spirit moves them to choose.

**Position B:** the Spirit's grace can be resisted and rejected by any person; if it could not be, the universal offer of salvation and the genuine conditionality of repentance and faith would be illusory.

### Perseverance of the Saints

**Position A:** all those who are truly elect and regenerate will persevere in faith to the end; true apostasy — final falling away from genuine salvation — is impossible. The warning passages of Hebrews address the danger of remaining in an unregenerate profession, not the loss of genuine salvation.

**Position B:** genuine believers can apostatize; the New Testament warning passages address the real possibility that those who have truly believed may fall away. Perseverance is the normal outcome of genuine faith sustained by grace, but it is not guaranteed unconditionally.

### Prevenient Grace

**Position A:** prevenient grace as a distinct and universal category prior to election is not a Pauline doctrine; it is a theological construction introduced to preserve libertarian freedom within a broadly monergist framework.

**Position B:** prevenient grace is the gracious work of the Spirit that precedes conversion in every person to whom the gospel comes, restoring sufficient freedom to respond and removing the excuse of inability. It is grounded in 1 Timothy 2:4, John 1:9, John 6:44, and the universal offer of the gospel.

## Foundational Assumptions

### Position A assumes

Calvinists begin with the conviction that God's sovereignty in salvation is absolute: if God wills the salvation of a person, that person will be saved. A further assumption is that divine glory is most fully displayed when salvation is entirely God's work from election through glorification, with no ultimate causal contribution from the creature. A third assumption is that the weight of the Pauline texts on election — particularly Romans 8:29–30 and Ephesians 1:4–5 — establishes individual, unconditional predestination as the framework within which all other soteriology must be read.

### Position B assumes

Arminians begin with the conviction that God genuinely wills the salvation of every person (1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9) and that this universal will is not a merely general wish but an actual provision. A further assumption is that moral responsibility requires genuine ability: if a person cannot respond to the gospel, commands to repent and believe are not meaningful commands. A third assumption is that election is relational and corporate before it is individual: God elects in Christ, and individuals participate in election by being in Christ through faith.

# Position A: Calvinism: Strongest Case

---

## Argument 1: Total Depravity and the Noetic Effects of Sin

Calvinism begins with the exegetical claim that sin has not merely weakened the human will but has bound it. Romans 8:7–8 states that the mind set on the flesh is hostile to God, does not submit to God's law, and indeed cannot. Romans 3:10–12 draws on Psalm 14 to state that no one seeks God. John 6:44 states that no one can come to the Son unless the Father draws him. These are not statements about impaired performance but about categorical inability apart from divine initiative.

> **P1:** Romans 8:7–8 states that the carnal mind cannot submit to God and cannot please him.
>
> **P2:** Romans 3:10–12 states that no one seeks God apart from divine initiative.
>
> **P3:** If the unregenerate person categorically cannot come to God, the grace that brings anyone to salvation must be prior to and the cause of that coming, not its response.
>
> **C:** Human depravity requires an effectual divine grace that precedes and produces faith, not one that merely enables it.

## Argument 2: Unconditional Election in Romans 8–9

The Pauline doctrine of election in Romans 8–9 does not ground God's choice in foreseen faith. Romans 9:11–13 explicitly says God's purpose in election operates before the children had done anything good or bad, so that it might continue not because of works but because of him who calls. The purpose is God's; the basis is God's will, not anything in Jacob or Esau. Romans 8:29–30 presents a golden chain — foreknew, predestined, called, justified, glorified — in which each link is effectually connected and no individual is lost along the way.

> **P1:** Romans 9:11–13 grounds the election of Jacob over Esau in God's sovereign purpose, not in foreseen merit or response.
>
> **P2:** Romans 8:29–30 presents a sequence in which everyone foreknown is ultimately glorified, with no losses between links.
>
> **P3:** If election is conditioned on foreseen faith, the question of why one person has faith and another does not is only pushed back a level, and the ultimate explanation remains God's prior knowledge, not the person's own contribution.
>
> **C:** Unconditional election is the most natural reading of Paul's argument in Romans 8–9, and it is the reading Paul himself gives to the objection that God is unjust (Romans 9:14–18).

## Argument 3: Irresistible Grace and John 6

John 6:37–44 presents a sequence: the Father gives specific persons to the Son; those given will come; those who come, the Son will not cast out; none given by the Father will be lost. The drawing in 6:44 is particular — it is the Father drawing specific people — and Jesus connects it directly to resurrection on the last day, meaning those drawn arrive at glorification. The movement from giving to coming to keeping is effectual throughout.

> **P1:** John 6:37 says the Father gives specific people to the Son, and all given will come.
>
> **P2:** John 6:44 says no one can come unless the Father draws him, and those drawn arrive at resurrection.
>
> **P3:** If the drawing is effectual — all given come; all who come are kept — then the grace producing the coming cannot be ultimately resistible without breaking the chain Jesus describes.
>
> **C:** The Johannine teaching on drawing and coming supports an irresistible, effectual grace given to the elect.

## Argument 4: Perseverance and the Golden Chain

The assurance of final salvation rests on its being entirely God's work. Romans 8:29–30 presents the chain from foreknowledge to glorification with all links intact. John 10:28–29 says the sheep will never perish and that no one can snatch them from the Father's hand. Philippians 1:6 says that God who began a good work in believers will bring it to completion. If any link in that chain could be broken by human decision, the assurance Paul grounds in God's purpose would be illusory.

> **P1:** Romans 8:29–30 presents all foreknown individuals as eventually glorified, with no dropout.
>
> **P2:** John 10:28–29 grounds the sheep's security in the Father's hold, which no external or internal force can break.
>
> **P3:** An assurance grounded entirely in divine faithfulness cannot be undermined by human failure without making that assurance dependent on the very thing it is meant to transcend.
>
> **C:** The perseverance of the saints follows from the divine sovereignty that grounds unconditional election, irresistible grace, and effectual calling.

## Position A: Critique of Position B

### Critique 1: Prevenient Grace Is Exegetically Underdetermined

The Arminian system depends on prevenient grace restoring universal libertarian ability, but no passage explicitly teaches that the Spirit gives such grace to every human being. Arminians derive it from the universal salvific will passages and from the logic of moral responsibility. Calvinists argue that the universal salvific will does not require universal restoration of libertarian freedom; it may simply mean that God genuinely offers salvation on terms that would be met if they were met, which is a real offer even without universal enabling grace.

> **P1:** No New Testament text explicitly teaches that the Spirit gives a prevenient grace universally restoring libertarian free will before election.
>
> **P2:** The inference from universal salvific will to universal enabling grace is a theological construction, not an exegetical derivation.
>
> **P3:** The passages Arminians cite for prevenient grace (John 1:9; John 6:44; Titus 2:11) can be read within a Calvinist framework without implying universal restoration of libertarian ability.
>
> **C:** Prevenient grace as a theological mechanism is an extrabiblical construct assembled to preserve human freedom rather than derived from the text.

### Critique 2: Conditional Election Makes Election Depend on Foreknowledge of Human Choice

If God elects on the basis of foreseen faith, then the ultimate determinant of who is saved is the human choice that God foreknows. God's election then ratifies what he knew the person would freely do. But this means the decisive factor in salvation is the human being's libertarian choice, and God's election is effectively a rubber stamp. That inverts the Pauline logic of Romans 9, where Paul insists the purpose of election is that it not depend on anything in the person.

> **P1:** If election is conditioned on foreseen faith, the deciding factor for each person's eternal destiny is that person's libertarian choice.
>
> **P2:** God's eternal decree then follows and confirms what he foreknew would happen, rather than determining what will happen.
>
> **P3:** Romans 9:11–13 explicitly excludes conditioning election on anything in the person, and verse 16 says it depends not on human will or exertion but on God who has mercy.
>
> **C:** Conditional election based on foreseen faith contradicts Paul's explicit argument that election operates apart from any factor in the person.

### Critique 3: Universal Atonement Creates a Logically Unstable System

If Christ died for all persons without exception, and if his death was a genuine substitutionary payment for sin, then all are acquitted and universal salvation follows. To avoid universalism, Arminians must qualify the substitutionary nature of the atonement: Christ's death provides a basis for forgiveness rather than accomplishing forgiveness. Calvinists argue that definite atonement maintains the substitutionary integrity of Christ's death by matching its design to its actual effectiveness.

> **P1:** If Christ's death is a genuine substitutionary payment for each person's sins, and it was made for all, then all are pardoned and hell is unjust.
>
> **P2:** To escape universalism, Arminian atonement theology must treat Christ's death as providing potential rather than accomplishing actual redemption, which qualifies its substitutionary character.
>
> **P3:** Definite atonement maintains substitutionary integrity by limiting the design of the atonement to those for whom it is applied.
>
> **C:** Universal atonement as a strict doctrine creates a logical pressure toward universalism that Arminian theology can only resolve by weakening substitution.

## Position A: Rebuttals to Position B

### Rebuttal 1: 1 Timothy 2:4 and 2 Peter 3:9 Revisited

Arminians cite 1 Timothy 2:4 ("God desires all people to be saved") and 2 Peter 3:9 ("not wishing that any should perish") as evidence of a universal salvific will incompatible with unconditional election. Calvinists respond with two readings. First, "all people" in 1 Timothy 2 may mean all kinds of people — kings and those in high positions, as the immediate context specifies — not each individual without exception. Second, 2 Peter 3:9 says God is patient "toward you" (the elect addressees), not wishing that any of them should perish, pointing to a specific group rather than all humanity.

> **P1:** 1 Timothy 2:1–4 uses "all people" in a context immediately specifying kings and those in authority, suggesting "all kinds" rather than each individual.
>
> **P2:** 2 Peter 3:9 addresses the elect ("toward you"), meaning God's patience aims at their full number coming to repentance, not that he wishes every individual in history to be saved.
>
> **P3:** Both texts can be read consistently with unconditional election without requiring that God has an unfulfilled salvific will toward the reprobate.
>
> **C:** The universal-will passages do not require the Arminian interpretation of a frustrated universal salvific intent.

### Rebuttal 2: The Warning Passages Revisited

Arminians argue that the warning passages of Hebrews (6:4–6; 10:26–31) demonstrate that genuine believers can fall away. Calvinists respond with two strategies. Some argue that the passages describe people with genuine spiritual experiences who were never regenerate (the non-perseverance of false professors). Others, following John Owen, argue the warnings function as the means by which God preserves the elect: the warning is real and addresses a real danger, but the elect will not in fact fulfill its condition because God's grace will sustain them.

> **P1:** The warning passages may describe spiritually experienced persons who nonetheless were never regenerate, as the absence of the normal perseverance markers supports.
>
> **P2:** Alternatively, warning passages function as instruments of preservation: the threat is real, but the elect respond to it by clinging to Christ, so the condition is never fulfilled.
>
> **P3:** Neither reading requires that a genuinely regenerate, elect person can finally fall away.
>
> **C:** The warning passages are consistent with Calvinist perseverance under either the false-professor or the means-of-preservation reading.

# Position B: Arminianism: Strongest Case

---

## Argument 1: God Wills the Salvation of All People

The clearest starting point for Arminianism is the cluster of texts asserting God's universal salvific will. 1 Timothy 2:4 says God desires all people to be saved and to come to a knowledge of the truth. 2 Peter 3:9 says the Lord is not willing that any should perish but that all should reach repentance. Ezekiel 18:23 and 33:11 say God takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked. If God genuinely wills the salvation of every individual and yet most are not saved, something in the creature's free response, not God's sovereign will, explains the difference.

> **P1:** 1 Timothy 2:4 states God's desire that all persons be saved in a context that gives no grammatical signal of restriction.
>
> **P2:** Ezekiel 18:23 and 33:11 express God's stated aversion to the death of the wicked and his active desire for their repentance, without qualification to a subset.
>
> **P3:** If God genuinely wills the salvation of all and is omnipotent, the explanation for those not saved must lie in a creaturely factor God has sovereignly chosen to allow — namely, resistible free response.
>
> **C:** The universal salvific will passages require a salvation mechanism that leaves room for genuine creaturely response to explain differential outcomes.

## Argument 2: Genuine Conditionality of Repentance and Faith

The New Testament presents repentance and faith as genuine conditions of salvation addressed to all hearers. Acts 17:30 says God commands all people everywhere to repent. John 3:16 says that whoever believes will have eternal life. Mark 1:15 presents the imperative to repent and believe as an address to the hearers of the kingdom. These commands are not rhetorical; they are real calls to real people. If those people cannot respond without a grace that only the elect receive, the universal command is either deceptive or meaningless for most of those who hear it.

> **P1:** Repentance and faith are commanded of all hearers without qualification as to whether the hearer possesses enabling grace.
>
> **P2:** A genuine command requires genuine ability on the part of the one commanded; otherwise the command is a performative contradiction.
>
> **P3:** If only the elect receive the grace enabling genuine response, the universal command is addressed to many for whom it cannot function as a real imperative.
>
> **C:** The genuine universality of the gospel command requires that all who hear the gospel possess sufficient grace to respond.

## Argument 3: Corporate Election in Christ

Ephesians 1:4 says God chose us in him before the foundation of the world. The prepositional phrase is load-bearing: election is in Christ, meaning Christ is the elect one and believers participate in election by being in him. Romans 8:29 says those God foreknew he predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son; the foreknowledge is relational and corporate. The Arminian reading does not make God a passive observer of human choices; it means God determined to save all who are in Christ, and individuals enter that elected body through faith.

> **P1:** Ephesians 1:4 locates the election of believers in Christ, suggesting that the locus of election is the corporate body united to the elect one.
>
> **P2:** Individuals participate in the election of the body by union with Christ through faith, which is the mechanism of entry into the elect body.
>
> **P3:** Romans 8:29's foreknowledge is relational — God knowing persons in covenant relationship — rather than simple precognition of abstract future choices.
>
> **C:** Corporate election in Christ gives God full sovereignty over the plan of salvation while making individual participation depend on a genuine faith-response.

## Argument 4: The Warning Passages Require Genuine Apostasy Risk

Hebrews 6:4–6 describes persons who have been enlightened, tasted the heavenly gift, shared in the Holy Spirit, tasted the goodness of the Word, and experienced the powers of the age to come, and then fallen away. The description matches genuine Christian experience at every point. Hebrews 10:26–31 warns of a fearful judgment for those who continue sinning after receiving knowledge of the truth. 2 Peter 2:20–22 describes people who have escaped the defilements of the world through knowledge of the Lord Jesus Christ and then become entangled again. If these are never-regenerate persons, the warnings lose their address to genuine believers.

> **P1:** Hebrews 6:4–6 describes the spiritual profile of genuine Christian experience in detail; reading it as describing the non-regenerate requires treating all those markers as simulacra.
>
> **P2:** If the warning passages address non-regenerate professors, they provide no warning for genuine believers, which removes their function within the pastoral letter.
>
> **P3:** 2 Peter 2:20–22 uses the language of escape, knowledge, and entanglement in a way that fits the pattern of genuine conversion followed by apostasy.
>
> **C:** The warning passages address the genuine possibility of final apostasy in real believers and require an Arminian (or at least non-Calvinist-perseverance) reading to serve their actual function in their letters.

## Position B: Critique of Position A

### Critique 1: The Calvinist God and Moral Responsibility

If God's sovereign, irresistible grace is the sole cause of anyone's salvation, and if God withholds that grace from the reprobate while commanding them to repent, the reprobate are commanded to do something they have no ability to do and face eternal punishment for failing to do it. The command has no genuine address to their will. Calvinists respond that the demand is grounded in what they ought to be (created in God's image) rather than what they are (fallen). Arminians find this response exegetically unsatisfying: commands function as real imperatives, and real imperatives presuppose genuine addressability.

> **P1:** If the unregenerate genuinely cannot repent and believe without irresistible grace, and if that grace is withheld from the reprobate, the command to repent is not a genuine imperative for those persons.
>
> **P2:** Commands addressed to persons who cannot comply and will be punished for not complying raise coherence problems about the justice of that punishment.
>
> **P3:** Arminians argue that moral responsibility requires sufficient ability, and that the gospel offer requires genuine addressability of the will.
>
> **C:** Unconditional reprobation, combined with genuine commands to all hearers, creates a justice problem the Calvinist system must address without simply invoking divine mystery.

### Critique 2: Romans 9 Is About Corporate or National Election, Not Individual Salvation

Arminians argue that Calvinists import the category of individual election to salvation into Romans 9, but the chapter's actual argument concerns the basis for God's dealing with Israel versus Gentiles in redemptive history. Jacob and Esau represent peoples (nations), as 9:12 states: "the older will serve the younger" refers to the nations of Israel and Edom, not to the eternal destinies of individuals. Pharaoh is hardened as an instrument in the Exodus narrative — a historical role in redemptive history — not an example of an individual predestined to hell.

> **P1:** Romans 9:12 ("the older will serve the younger") cites Genesis 25:23, which explicitly concerns nations, not individuals.
>
> **P2:** The Pharaoh reference concerns God's use of Pharaoh as an instrument in the Exodus narrative, not Pharaoh's individual eternal destiny.
>
> **P3:** The question Paul is answering (Romans 9:6) is whether God's word to Israel has failed — a question about corporate covenant faithfulness, not individual election to salvation.
>
> **C:** Romans 9 addresses the question of God's faithfulness in his covenant purposes with Israel and the Gentiles, not a general doctrine of unconditional individual election to salvation.

### Critique 3: Double Predestination and the Character of God

If unconditional election is correct, God passes over the reprobate and — on the supralapsarian reading — actively ordains their damnation for his glory. This creates a picture of God deliberately creating persons for the purpose of condemning them. Even infralapsarian Calvinists, who treat reprobation as passing over rather than actively ordaining, face the question of why God would create persons he knew would inevitably sin and be damned if he could have elected them instead. Arminians argue that this picture conflicts with God's character as revealed in Ezekiel 18 and 1 Timothy 2.

> **P1:** Unconditional reprobation means God decided before creation not to give the reprobate the grace he knew was necessary for their salvation.
>
> **P2:** Ezekiel 18:23 and 33:11 assert God's genuine non-pleasure in the death of the wicked and his desire for their repentance.
>
> **P3:** A God who takes no pleasure in the wicked's death but also chose before creation not to provide the grace needed for their repentance presents an incoherence in divine motivation.
>
> **C:** Unconditional reprobation sits in tension with the revealed character of God as one who genuinely desires the wicked to turn and live.

## Position B: Rebuttals to Position A

### Rebuttal 1: Foreknowledge Is Relational, Not Simple Prescience

Calvinists argue that conditional election based on foreseen faith makes God's election dependent on the creature's choice. Arminians respond that this misunderstands what foreknowledge means. God's foreknowledge is not passive awareness of decisions made independently of him; it is his relational knowing of persons who will be in covenant relationship with him through Christ. The order is: God determines that salvation will be through faith in Christ; God knows who will believe; God elects those he knows will be in Christ. The initiative and the terms are entirely God's; only the response is the creature's.

> **P1:** New Testament foreknowledge (proginosko) carries relational force in contexts like Romans 8:29 and 11:2, meaning "to know in advance in covenant relationship," not merely "to have advance information about."
>
> **P2:** God sovereignly determines the terms of salvation (faith in Christ), which means the framework of election remains entirely God's work.
>
> **P3:** Foreknowing who will respond within God's sovereign framework is not the same as God's election being determined by an independent human decision.
>
> **C:** Conditional election based on relational foreknowledge preserves divine initiative and sovereignty over the structure of salvation while allowing genuine creaturely response.

### Rebuttal 2: John 6 Does Not Require Irresistible Grace

Calvinists read John 6:37–44 as a chain from divine giving to certain coming. Arminians respond that the giving language describes God's provision and purpose, not the mechanism of individual regeneration. "All that the Father gives me will come to me" describes the reliability of God's purpose from his side: everyone the Father intends to give will in fact come, because the provision in Christ is adequate and the offer is genuine. The drawing in 6:44 is universal in John's Gospel context: 12:32 says "I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself," using the same verb. Drawing does not equal irresistible coercion.

> **P1:** John 12:32 uses the same "draw" verb (helkuō) of Christ drawing all people to himself through his lifting up, which in context describes universal gospel proclamation rather than selective irresistible grace.
>
> **P2:** If drawing is universal in 12:32, the drawing in 6:44 need not be a selective irresistible act but rather the universal appeal of the gospel through the Spirit.
>
> **P3:** The reliability of those given coming to the Son reflects God's purpose and provision, not necessarily a mechanism of overriding individual will.
>
> **C:** John 6 describes the reliability of God's salvific purpose and the effectiveness of Christ's work, not the irresistibility of grace as the means by which individuals come.

### Rebuttal 3: Romans 9 and the Corporate Reading

Calvinists argue that Romans 9:11–13 excludes any conditional basis for election. Arminians respond that the Jacob/Esau text in Genesis concerns national election (Genesis 25:23 explicitly says "two nations") and that Paul is arguing about God's prerogative to assign covenant roles and historical functions to peoples, not about the mechanism by which individuals are chosen for eternal salvation. God's freedom to assign Gentiles to covenant inclusion without their first becoming Jews is the argument Paul needs for Romans 9–11; individual unconditional election is not the argument the chapter's logic requires.

> **P1:** Genesis 25:23, cited in Romans 9:12, is explicitly about nations: "Two nations are in your womb."
>
> **P2:** Paul's question in Romans 9:6 — has God's word to Israel failed? — is about covenant faithfulness to a corporate entity, which a corporate reading of election in the chapter addresses.
>
> **P3:** God's freedom to harden Pharaoh as an instrument in Exodus and to include Gentiles in covenant blessing without Mosaic mediation is precisely the prerogative Paul needs to establish; individual unconditional election to salvation is not required for Paul's argument to succeed.
>
> **C:** The corporate-election reading of Romans 9 resolves Paul's argument about God's faithfulness to his covenant word without requiring that the chapter teach individual unconditional predestination to salvation.

## Spectrum & Synthesis (The Third Way)

*(The rigid Calvinist/Arminian binary maps the historic TULIP vs. Remonstrance debate beautifully, but it artificially flattens the broader historical Christian tradition. Several major theological frameworks refuse the premises of this A/B constraint entirely, proving that the mystery of salvation cannot be strictly toggled into two columns.)*

*   **Lutheran Soteriology (Single Predestination):** Historic Lutheranism breaks the Reformed logical symmetry. It firmly holds to unconditional election, total depravity, and monergistic grace (like Calvinism), affirming that dead sinners cannot choose God on their own. However, it explicitly rejects double predestination and limited atonement, insisting that Christ died universally for all and that God's grace *can* be resisted and finally lost (like Arminianism). It rests in the paradox of divine sovereignty and human culpability without demanding logical resolution.
*   **Eastern Orthodox *Synergia*:** The Eastern church rejects the Western Augustinian framing of original guilt entirely, breaking the presuppositions of both Calvinists and Arminians. Instead of viewing salvation primarily through a forensic, courtroom lens of legal declarations and divine decrees, Orthodoxy views salvation as *theosis*—a cooperative healing process where humanity's wounded (but not completely destroyed) image is restored through cooperative union with God's uncreated energies.
*   **Amyraldism (Four-Point Calvinism / Hypothetical Universalism):** Originating in the French Reformed Academy of Saumur, this framework retains the Calvinist doctrines of total depravity, unconditional election, irresistible grace, and perseverance of the saints. However, it completely breaks the TULIP acronym by replacing Limited Atonement with Unlimited Atonement. It argues that Christ functionally died for the sins of all humanity, making salvation objectively possible for all, while God sovereignly and effectually applies that atonement only to the elect. This arguably represents the actual functional theology of a massive portion of modern evangelicals.

## The Three Actual Hinges

The first hinge is the **referent and mechanism of election in Romans 8–9 and Ephesians 1**. Calvinists read these texts as teaching individual, unconditional predestination to salvation. Arminians read them as teaching corporate election in Christ with individuals participating through faith. The resolution depends on whether "in him" in Ephesians 1:4 modifies the act of choosing or the sphere of election, and whether Romans 9's argument concerns individual eternal destinies or national covenant roles in redemptive history.

The second hinge is the **relationship between divine sovereignty and human freedom**. Calvinists hold that God's sovereignty in salvation requires effectual grace that produces what it intends. Arminians hold that genuine moral responsibility requires libertarian freedom, and that God sovereignly created a world in which he allows genuine creaturely response without that compromising his ultimate purposes. Neither side denies that both divine sovereignty and human responsibility are asserted in Scripture; they dispute the mechanism by which both are preserved.

The third hinge is the **universality of the salvific will and what it implies**. Calvinists either restrict the referent of "all" in the relevant texts or distinguish between God's revealed/preceptive will and his decretive will, so that he genuinely commands all to repent without his decretive will being that all will repent. Arminians treat these texts as expressing a genuine divine intention for every individual's salvation, which requires a salvation mechanism that allows for genuine creaturely participation in the outcome.

## Calvinist / Reformed

Calvin, John. *Institutes of the Christian Religion*. Edited by John T. McNeill. Translated by Ford Lewis Battles. 2 vols. Philadelphia: Westminster, 1960. The primary systematic source for the Reformed doctrine of election and predestination.

Owen, John. *The Death of Death in the Death of Christ*. Edinburgh: Banner of Truth, 1959. The most thorough classical defense of definite atonement; includes arguments from the logic of substitution and the divine intention.

Piper, John. *The Justification of God: An Exegetical and Theological Study of Romans 9:1–23*. 2nd ed. Grand Rapids: Baker, 1993. Detailed exegetical defense of unconditional individual election in Romans 9; engages Arminian alternatives.

Schreiner, Thomas R., and Bruce A. Ware, eds. *The Grace of God, the Bondage of the Will*. 2 vols. Grand Rapids: Baker, 1995. Multi-author Calvinist response to I. Howard Marshall and others; covers Romans 9, John 6, and Ephesians 1 in detail.

Berkhof, Louis. *Systematic Theology*. Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1941. Standard Reformed systematic treatment of election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, and perseverance.

Murray, John. *Redemption Accomplished and Applied*. Grand Rapids: Eerdmans, 1955. Concise Reformed account of the ordo salutis; treats definite atonement and effectual calling.

Sproul, R. C. *Chosen by God*. Wheaton, IL: Tyndale House, 1986. Accessible Reformed exposition of election and predestination; widely used at the popular-scholarly level.

Warfield, B. B. *The Plan of Salvation*. Philadelphia: Presbyterian Board of Publication, 1915. Short systematic defense of the Reformed doctrine of salvation; treats the relation of divine sovereignty and human agency.

Canons of Dort (1619). The confessional statement of the Synod of Dort; defines the Five Points of Calvinism (TULIP) in response to the Arminian Remonstrance.

## Arminian / Non-Calvinist

Arminius, James. *The Works of James Arminius*. 3 vols. Translated by James Nichols and William Nichols. London, 1825–1828. Reprint, Grand Rapids: Baker, 1986. Primary source for Arminian theology; includes his disputations, orations, and correspondence on election and grace.

The Five Arminian Articles (Remonstrance, 1610). The original Arminian statement submitted to the Dutch Reformed Church; defines the five disputed points against Calvinist orthodoxy.

Olson, Roger E. *Arminian Theology: Myths and Realities*. Downers Grove, IL: IVP Academic, 2006. The most comprehensive contemporary defense of classical Arminianism; corrects common Calvinist misreadings of Arminius and his tradition.

Marshall, I. Howard. *Kept by the Power of God: A Study of Perseverance and Falling Away*. Minneapolis: Bethany Fellowship, 1969. Exegetical case that the New Testament teaches the genuine possibility of apostasy; covers Hebrews, John, and Paul.

Pinnock, Clark H., ed. *The Grace of God and the Will of Man*. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 1989. Multi-author Arminian response to Calvinist exegesis; engages Romans 9, John 6, and Ephesians 1.

Wiley, H. Orton. *Christian Theology*. 3 vols. Kansas City: Beacon Hill, 1940–1943. Standard Wesleyan-Arminian systematic theology; treats prevenient grace, conditional election, and unlimited atonement in detail.

Forlines, F. Leroy. *The Quest for Truth: Theology for Postmodern Times*. Nashville: Randall House, 2001. Classical Arminian systematics with careful treatment of depravity, election, and prevenient grace.

Klein, William W. *The New Chosen People: A Corporate View of Election*. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 1990. Exegetical case for the corporate reading of election in the Pauline epistles; treats "in Christ" language in Ephesians 1.

## Four Views / Crossover

Schreiner, Thomas R., and Bruce A. Ware, eds. *Still Sovereign: Contemporary Perspectives on Election, Foreknowledge, and Grace*. Grand Rapids: Baker, 2000. Calvinist essays responding to post-Arminian and open theist alternatives.

Caneday, A. B., and Thomas R. Schreiner. *The Race Set Before Us: A Biblical Theology of Perseverance and Assurance*. Downers Grove, IL: InterVarsity Press, 2001. Calvinist treatment of the warning passages as means of preservation.

McKnight, Scot. "The Warning Passages of Hebrews: A Formal Analysis and Theological Conclusions." *Trinity Journal* 13 (1992): 21–59. Surveys major interpretive positions on the Hebrews warnings; neither straightforwardly Calvinist nor Arminian.

Ashby, Stephen M. "A Reformed Arminian View." In *Four Views on Eternal Security*, edited by J. Matthew Pinson, 137–85. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 2002. Classical Arminian entry in the four-views format; treats perseverance and the warning passages.

Pinson, J. Matthew, ed. *Four Views on Eternal Security*. Grand Rapids: Zondervan, 2002. Contributors represent classical Calvinist, moderate Calvinist, Reformed Arminian, and Wesleyan-Arminian positions.

Geisler, Norman L. *Chosen But Free*. 3rd ed. Minneapolis: Bethany House, 2010. Moderate Calvinist (four-point) alternative; critiques both strict Calvinism and Arminianism; engages both sets of key texts.
